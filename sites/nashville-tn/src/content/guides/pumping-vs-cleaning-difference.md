## The two jobs

Most companies use the words interchangeably. They describe genuinely different work.

### Pumping

The tank is emptied with a vacuum hose: the liquid, the floating scum layer and the settled sludge at the bottom, followed by a backflush. This is the routine service most households need every three to five years.

**$300–$525** for a typical residential tank.

### Cleaning

Everything in a pump-out, plus two additional things a hose alone cannot do:

- **Mechanical break-up** of a hardened scum mat or compacted sludge that the hose cannot lift
- **Wash-down of the interior walls and floor**, so the concrete, both baffles and the effluent filter can actually be seen rather than sitting under a coating of residue

**$400–$650**, and the difference is time on site rather than different equipment.

## The distinction that actually matters

Here is the part worth understanding, because it is where cheap quotes hide.

A **liquid draw-down** is neither of the above. The hose takes the liquid, the tank level drops dramatically, and it looks emptied — while the settled sludge, which is the material that will eventually reach your drain field, stays exactly where it was.

That is not pumping. It is a partial job that leaves the actual problem in place, and from the surface you cannot tell the difference. This is the single most common way a cheaper quote is cheaper.

So the more useful comparison is not pumping versus cleaning. It is **complete removal versus partial removal**, and every quote should be checked against that first.

## Which do you need?

**Routine pumping is enough if:**

- You have pumped on schedule and have records
- Measured sludge and scum depths are within normal range
- No long gap in the service history
- The tank has been internally inspected in recent years

**Cleaning earns its cost if:**

- Intervals have been missed for years, or the tank has never been pumped
- The scum mat has hardened enough that a hose cannot break it
- Sludge has compacted at the tank floor
- You have bought a property with no records and want the tank properly assessed rather than guessed at
- Odour or symptoms persist after a routine pump-out

Broadly, most households need pumping on a cycle and a cleaning every second or third visit — more often where intervals have slipped.

## Why a clean tank inspects better

This is the underrated benefit. Baffles and tank walls sit under residue after a pump-out. Wash that down and you can see:

- Cracks and spalling in the tank walls
- The actual condition of both baffles rather than their approximate shape
- Root intrusion at joints
- Groundwater infiltration
- Whether an effluent filter is present, and its real condition

For a property with unknown history, a cleaning is as much a diagnostic as a service. On a system 25 to 55 years old — which describes most tanks in Davidson County's unsewered areas — that assessment is worth having before deciding anything else.

## How to tell you are not being upsold

One question: **"What were the measured sludge and scum depths, and had the scum mat hardened?"**

A company that measures answers immediately. One working from a script cannot. If you have pumped on schedule and have records, the honest answer is usually that pumping is all you need — and you should expect to be told that.
