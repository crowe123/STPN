## The rule

In Tennessee it is illegal to advertise a home for more bedrooms than it is septic-approved for. If a property is approved for fewer bedrooms than advertised, it cannot be advertised with the higher number — and the person advertising it is liable for misrepresentation.

The obligation sits with whoever puts the listing out. That is worth stating plainly, because the usual situation is an owner who did not build the extra room and had no idea the rule existed.

## Why the count exists at all

Septic systems are sized by bedroom count, used as a proxy for daily wastewater flow. Tank capacity and drain field area are calculated from it at permitting.

So the number is not a description of the house. It is the **design capacity of the wastewater system**, and it is the figure the county holds on record.

## Where the mismatch comes from

Rarely from anyone deceptive. Usually from time.

A three-bedroom house from 1978 gets an attic conversion in 1996. A basement is finished with a guest room in 2004. A garage becomes a fourth bedroom in 2015. Nobody involved thought about the septic permit, and the system was never upgraded to match. Thirty years later it is listed as five bedrooms and the approval still says three.

Meanwhile the system has been running above its design flow for decades, which is often why the drain field is in the state it is in.

## How to find your number

Metro Public Health holds it for Davidson County. Their "How Many Bedrooms?" service returns the approval date, inspection dates and the approved bedroom count for an existing residence.

**Environmental Engineering Services: 615-340-5604.** You will need the property's map and parcel number.

## If your count is short

**Do not simply list it anyway.** The exposure is real and the problem does not stay hidden — a buyer's septic inspection is exactly where it surfaces, at the worst possible moment in the transaction.

Your realistic options:

**Upgrade the system.** Increasing the approved count means permitting: a site evaluation including a soil morphology assessment by a soil scientist, and enough suitable area on the parcel. On slow-draining Middle Tennessee soils, and on lots with limited reserve area, this is not always possible.

**Advertise to the approval.** Take advice from your agent on describing the space accurately without claiming a bedroom count the system does not support.

**Disclose.** Tennessee requires disclosure of known septic details anyway, and a buyer who knows what they are getting is a much smaller problem than one who finds out afterwards.

## For short-term rental operators

The rule is written about advertising, and a short-term rental listing is advertising. Nashville has one of the country's largest STR markets, and listings routinely advertise sleeping capacity well beyond the permitted bedroom count.

Beyond the legal question, the practical one is unavoidable: a system permitted for three bedrooms and loaded by ten guests on a peak weekend is running at several times design flow. That shows up as weekend slow drains first, and a failed field later.

## Bottom line

Get the number from Metro before you list or let the property. If the house has outgrown the approval, take advice rather than hoping — it is the single most common finding in pre-sale septic inspections. Call **(615) 234-9048** for an inspection that checks the record as standard.
