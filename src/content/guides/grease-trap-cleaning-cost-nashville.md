## Why there is no published band

Residential septic pumping has a published statewide range because the jobs are broadly similar. Grease interceptors are not: capacity spans from a small under-sink unit to a several-thousand-gallon in-ground interceptor, intervals range from monthly to annual, and access varies from a lid in an open service yard to one under a working kitchen floor.

Price follows those three variables. What can be stated up front is how the interval is set, because that is what determines annual cost far more than the per-visit figure does.

## The quarter-full rule

The working benchmark across the trade is that an interceptor is due for service when fats, oils, grease plus settled solids reach roughly **25% of total capacity**.

The reasoning is retention time. An interceptor works by holding incoming flow long enough for grease to float and solids to settle. As the accumulated layers grow, the working volume shrinks, retention time drops, and grease starts passing through untrapped. Past the quarter mark the trap is progressively doing less of what it was installed to do.

Measuring against that benchmark on the first few visits gives a real accumulation rate for the site, and the interval follows from it.

## What a full clean includes, and what skimming leaves

A full clean removes the grease cap, the liquid, and the settled solids beneath it, scrapes interior walls where the cap has hardened, and inspects the inlet and outlet baffles and flow restrictor while the trap is empty.

Skimming removes the visible cap. It looks identical from the lid, costs marginally less, and leaves the settled solids that occupy the bottom of the working volume. The trap is back at its threshold within weeks. It is the same shortcut as pumping a septic tank without lifting the sludge layer, and it produces the same outcome: a site paying more often for less.

## What degreasers do to the economics

Enzyme and chemical degreasers are the most expensive thing in this article, and they do not appear on the interceptor invoice.

They emulsify grease so it flows down the line. From the kitchen's perspective that clears a slow drain. From the interceptor's perspective it defeats the mechanism entirely — emulsified grease does not separate and float, so it passes straight through and re-solidifies somewhere downstream. On a sewered site that is the lateral. On a septic site it is the tank and, eventually, the drain field.

The tell is a thin cap in the trap alongside a heavy load further along. Where that shows up, the real cost of the degreaser is the drain field, at $6,000 to $10,000 in Middle Tennessee.

## The paperwork is part of what you are buying

Tennessee permits septage and grease haulers through TDEC, and permitted vehicles must display the firm name, address and removal permit number in lettering at least two inches high on a contrasting background.

Each cleaning should produce a dated manifest: volume removed, disposal site, and trap condition. Health department inspections ask for it. So does an insurer after a loss, and so does a buyer's surveyor when the business changes hands. Keeping it is free; reconstructing it later is not possible.
