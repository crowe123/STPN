## The list, and what each one does

**Wipes, including flushable ones.** The label claim is that they clear the toilet. They do. What they do not do is break down, and in a septic tank they accumulate into mats that block the inlet and consume working volume. This is the most common avoidable finding in tanks installed in the last twenty years.

**Grease and cooking oil.** Poured down the kitchen sink it cools, solidifies, and joins the scum layer. Enough of it and the scum layer reaches the outlet baffle, at which point it starts leaving with the effluent and coating the drain field.

**Solvents, paint, thinners, petrol.** These kill the bacterial population the tank relies on and can pass through to the drain field and groundwater. On a property with a private well that is a drinking water issue.

**Heavy bleach and drain cleaner loads.** Ordinary household use is fine. Sustained heavy dosing or a large single slug suppresses the biology, and the tank treats less effectively while it recovers.

**Water softener regeneration discharge.** Widespread on wells across Joelton, Whites Creek and Bells Bend, where the water is hard. Every cycle adds a large volume of brine the field was never sized for, plus salinity that works against the bacteria. Rerouting it is usually simple and is one of the highest-value changes available on a well-and-septic property.

**Non-degradables generally** — sanitary products, cotton buds, dental floss, cat litter, cigarette ends. They occupy volume permanently until a truck removes them.

## The ones that are not about the drains

**Vehicles and trailers on the drain field.** Compaction crushes laterals and seals the soil interface. On Davidson County's clay soils, which are already slow to accept water, this does real and irreversible damage. It is common on Antioch and Una lots where a parking pad went in over an unmarked field footprint.

**Sheds, above-ground pools, raised beds** over the field, for the same reason plus the difficulty of ever getting at it again.

**Downspouts and driveway runoff** discharging across the field. On clay that holds water rather than shedding it, this is enough on its own to push a marginal field into seasonal failure — and on karst it is what concentrates infiltration and opens sinkholes.

**Tree planting near the tank or laterals.** Roots find penetrations and pipe joints. Root intrusion at the inlet is a frequent finding on the wooded lots through Forest Hills and Bellevue.

## What is fine

Normal toilet paper. Ordinary quantities of household cleaning products. Laundry, spread across the week rather than eight loads on a Saturday — the issue with laundry is hydraulic surge, not the detergent.

Showers, dishwashers, normal life. A septic system is designed for a household; it just is not designed for a household treating it as a waste disposal route for things that should go in the bin.

## Why this list matters more than any product

None of the above is solved by an additive. Nothing poured into a tank dissolves a wipe mat, un-compacts a drain field, or removes brine loading.

The two habits that actually determine how long a septic system lasts are keeping the list above out of it, and pumping on interval so that solids never reach the field. Everything else is secondary. A Middle Tennessee drain field replacement is commonly $6,000 to $10,000; the list is what stands between a household and that number.

## If you have just bought a septic property

Schedule a pump-out and inspection within 90 days of closing. The readings taken then describe the previous owner's habits, which is genuinely worth knowing — a tank three-quarters full of sludge after a stated five-year interval tells you something specific.

Wait two years and those readings blend the old owner's usage with yours, and the baseline is gone.
