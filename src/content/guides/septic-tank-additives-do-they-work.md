## What a tank actually needs to work

A septic tank is a settling chamber with a resident bacterial population that digests the organic fraction of what arrives. That population is seeded and continuously replenished by the waste itself. A household producing normal wastewater is inoculating its tank every day, at no cost, without thinking about it.

This is the fact most additive marketing quietly works around. The bacteria are not a consumable.

## The three product categories

**Biological additives** — bacteria, enzymes, yeast. Mostly harmless and mostly unnecessary. They add organisms to a system that is already producing its own, and the limiting factor in a septic tank is retention time and volume, not bacterial count.

**Inorganic chemicals** — acids, alkalis, strong oxidisers. These are the harmful ones. They can suppress or kill the bacterial population, and some corrode concrete tank components. A system recovering from a chemical dose treats less effectively while it does.

**Organic solvents and degreasers.** The worst category for a septic system, because they work exactly as advertised. They emulsify grease so it flows down the line. Emulsified grease does not float and does not get retained — it passes the tank and re-solidifies in the drain field, which is the component that cannot be serviced.

## What no additive can do

**Remove sludge.** Sludge is not purely organic. It contains the mineral and inorganic fraction of everything the household has sent down the drain, and there is no biological process that consumes that. It accumulates until a vacuum truck removes it.

**Restore a failed drain field.** Once the soil interface has been blinded by solids, the trench walls no longer accept water. Nothing poured into the tank travels to the field and reverses that. Products sold on this promise are the most expensive ones on the shelf, because they postpone a diagnosis while the field continues to fail.

**Fix a mechanical problem.** A missing outlet baffle, a blocked effluent filter, a collapsed inlet line, a cracked tank — all common findings, none of them chemical.

## Where the money is better spent

The same annual spend on additives buys, over a few years, most of a pump-out with a documented inspection. That visit produces things an additive cannot:

- Sludge and scum depths measured, giving a real interval for the specific household
- Baffle condition assessed with the tank empty, catching the failure that actually kills drain fields
- Effluent filter located and rinsed
- Tank integrity checked — which matters a great deal on a well property
- Written findings that work for an insurer or a future sale

## The one legitimate exception

Permitted alternative and engineered systems — aerobic treatment units, media filters, drip dispersal — sometimes carry manufacturer or permit conditions that specify particular products or dosing regimes. Where those conditions exist they are enforceable, and they should be followed.

That is a specific requirement attached to a specific permitted design. It is not an argument for pouring anything into a conventional gravity tank, which is what the great majority of Davidson County homes have.
