## The mismatch

A septic system is sized and permitted for a **bedroom count**, which is used as a proxy for daily wastewater flow from a household living there normally.

A short-term rental is not a household living there normally.

Nashville has one of the largest short-term rental markets in the country, and the loading pattern is distinctive: near-zero use midweek, then a weekend at maximum occupancy with a group behaving nothing like a resident family. More showers in a morning than the house would normally see in two days. Laundry between every changeover. Peak-hour simultaneous demand a three-bedroom design never contemplated.

The system does not care that the average across the month is fine. Drain fields fail on **peak hydraulic loading**, not on averages.

## Find your number first

Metro Public Health holds the approval record for Davidson County residences, including **the number of bedrooms the system is approved for**, along with approval and inspection dates.

**Environmental Engineering Services: 615-340-5604.** You will need the map and parcel number.

Compare that figure to what your listing advertises as sleeping capacity. If the listing says it sleeps ten and the approval says three bedrooms, that is the whole issue in one line.

## The legal dimension

Tennessee makes it **illegal to advertise a home for more bedrooms than its septic is approved for**, and whoever advertises it is liable for misrepresentation.

That rule is usually discussed around home sales. But it is written about **advertising**, and a short-term rental listing is advertising. If you are advertising bedroom counts above the approval, that is worth taking advice on before your next booking rather than after.

## What overloading actually does

The early signal is weekend-specific: drains slow on Saturday and Sunday, then behave perfectly by Wednesday. Owners frequently dismiss this as guests misusing the plumbing. It is usually the field failing to keep up with peak flow and catching up during the quiet days.

Then the recovery windows shorten. Then they stop.

Once the field's soil interface is sealed, you are into drain field territory: **$7,000 to $15,000 and up** in Tennessee, and toward the upper end here because Middle Tennessee's slow-draining karst soils require larger fields.

## Practical measures that help

**Pump on booking patterns, not the calendar.** Two to four times as often as a family home at high occupancy is normal.

**Service in the changeover gap.** A tank pumped between bookings costs you nothing in occupancy.

**Fit risers.** $300 to $600 once, and every future service becomes a fast two-minute open — which matters when you are working inside a four-hour turnover window.

**Tell guests about the septic.** A short, clear note in the property guide — nothing but toilet paper down the toilet, no wipes, spread the laundry — does more than owners expect. Most guests have never lived on a septic system and genuinely do not know.

**Have the field assessed before peak season,** not during it.

## Where this bites hardest in Davidson County

The septic-and-STR overlap sits in the areas the sewer never reached: Joelton, Whites Creek, the Bellevue and Pasquo side along SR-100, and out toward Bells Bend. Larger properties, rural settings, popular with groups — and every one of them on a system permitted for a family.
